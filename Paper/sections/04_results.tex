\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Low-dimensional consumer-resource dynamics predict combinatorial community assembly}
\label{subsec:venturelli}

To benchmark the predictive power of low-dimensional consumer-resource dynamics against combinatorial community assembly, we first analyzed an extensive synthetic human gut microbiome dataset comprising $S=12$ prevalent isolates cultured across 175 distinct consortia (monocultures, pairs, trios, and higher-order communities up to 12 species) sampled longitudinally across 6 timepoints over 60 hours (Venturelli et al.~\cite{venturelli2018deciphering}). Rather than fitting $S^2 = 144$ phenomenological interaction coefficients, our physical Neural ODE projects species interactions onto a low-dimensional latent metabolic space ($K \ll S$) governed by non-negative consumption affinities $\mathbf{\Theta}$, intraspecific crowding $\alpha$, and mass-conserving byproduct cross-feeding $W$ (Methods).

Cross-validation across 102 training communities selected an optimal architecture of $K = 3$ latent resources based on held-out simplex Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence ($0.2193 \pm 0.0085$ nats; Methods), reducing total parameters by $>59\%$ relative to unconstrained baselines (61 parameters vs. 152 for $K=7$).

Dashed line: identity. \textbf{b}, Trajectory prediction accuracy ($r$) versus initial Shannon diversity ($H$), showing no complexity-dependent decay ($r = -0.125,\ p = 0.292$). \label{fig:ophelia_assembly}

On 73 held-out test consortia across all dynamic timepoints ($t \in [12, 60]$\,h), the frozen $K=3$ model achieved high predictive accuracy (Fig.~\ref{fig:ophelia_assembly}a; test $R^2 = 0.766$, Pearson $r = 0.877$, $N = 4,380$; relative abundance $r = 0.899$). High accuracy was maintained across growth phases ($t = 24\text{h}: R^2 = 0.799$; $t = 60\text{h}: R^2 = 0.749$). Performance showed zero complexity-dependent decay against initial diversity ($r = -0.125,\ p = 0.292$; Fig.~\ref{fig:ophelia_assembly}b) and complete invariance to compositional distance from training consortia ($r = +0.036,\ p = 0.765$; Fig.~\ref{fig:ophelia_assembly}c). In a leave-pair-out benchmark withholding each species pair from training, the model reliably forecasted unobserved pairings (mean per-pair $R^2 = 0.874 \pm 0.061$, pooled $R^2 = 0.628$, $r = 0.806$; Methods).

\subsection{Universal metabolic traits enable zero-shot transfer across structured microenvironments}
\label{subsec:pollard}

Natural microbiomes organize as spatially structured metacommunities, partitioning across mucosal surfaces and luminal fluid while exchanging biomass~\cite{jin2023culturing}. To test whether low-dimensional consumer-resource dynamics generalize across physical microenvironments, we analyzed the defined 104-member synthetic human gut community (hCom2; Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2022design}, Jin et al.~\cite{jin2023culturing}) passaged over 6 serial dilution cycles across liquid broth (\textit{No Carrier}), inert hydrogel beads (\textit{Plain Agar}), and mucosal beads (\textit{Mucin Agar}). Following quality-control filtering to remove taxa with undetectable or negligible read counts across cycles (Methods), we modeled the dynamic trajectories of the 50 prevalent, persistently colonizing core strains.

We modeled each culture as a two-patch metacommunity linking the carrier biofilm ($c$) and liquid supernatant ($s$) via bidirectional attachment and shedding kinetics (Methods). Crucially, metabolic affinities $\mathbf{\Theta}$ and cross-feeding $W$ were constrained to be strictly identical across spatial compartments and regimes, attributing all environmental divergence to physical transport and dilution mechanics (Methods).

\caption{\textbf{Metacommunity CRM enables zero-shot transfer across physical microenvironments.} Models were trained on liquid (No Carrier) and mucosal (Mucin Agar) regimes, and evaluated zero-shot on inert Plain Agar across 6 serial dilution cycles ($K=4$). \textbf{a}, Zero-shot parity on supernatant broth ($R^2 = +0.289$, Pearson $r = 0.624$), colored by passage ($P_1 \to P_6$). \textbf{b}, Zero-shot parity on carrier biofilm ($R^2 = +0.442$, $r = 0.669$; Combined $R^2 = 0.386$, $r = 0.631$), outperforming the static inoculum null ($R^2 = -0.219$). \textbf{c}, Accuracy across passages $P_1 \to P_6$; after initial transient adjustment ($P_1 \to P_2$), equilibrium accuracy ($P_3 \to P_6$, yellow) stabilizes at $R^2 > 0.45$ ($r \approx 0.75$). \label{fig:pollard_plain_agar}
\end{figure*}

Trained solely on No Carrier and Mucin Agar ($K=4$; Methods), the frozen model predicted community assembly zero-shot on Plain Agar across all 6 serial passages (Fig.~\ref{fig:pollard_plain_agar}). The model generalized accurately across both niches (Fig.~\ref{fig:pollard_plain_agar}a--b): supernatant broth reached $R^2 = +0.289$ ($r = 0.624$) and carrier biofilm reached $R^2 = +0.442$ ($r = 0.669$; Combined $R^2 = 0.386$, $r = 0.631$), substantially exceeding the static inoculum null ($R^2 = -0.219$). Following initial transient adjustment ($P_1 \to P_2$), equilibrium zero-shot accuracy ($P_3 \to P_6$) stabilized at $R^2 > 0.45$ ($r \approx 0.75$; Fig.~\ref{fig:pollard_plain_agar}c), accurately capturing autonomous longitudinal succession among dominant colonizers such as \textit{Clostridium} sp. D5, \textit{Acidaminococcus intestini}, \textit{Mitsuokella multacida}, and \textit{Bacteroides uniformis} (Fig.~\ref{fig:pollard_plain_agar}d).

\subsection{Latent niche geometry and competitive pressure forecast host-specific intervention outcomes}
\label{subsec:german}

Microbiome interventions frequently exhibit unpredictable host-to-host variability, where identical treatments succeed in one host but fail in another. We investigated this in a livestock rumen microbiome dataset (Plata et al.; Methods) cultured under 5-bar syngas supplemented with Methanol ($\text{MeOH}$) or Trimethylamine ($\text{TMA}$), inoculated from two dairy cattle donors: Cow OSU and Cow BRRS. Following quality-control filtering to retain core resident taxa meeting longitudinal detection thresholds (Methods), we modeled the community dynamics across 81 prevalent bacterial and archaeal taxa. Under +TMA (5 bar), the keystone methanogen \textit{Methanomicrobium sp.} bloomed in Cow BRRS but was suppressed in Cow OSU.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/German_Theta_TMA_Niche_Competition.png}
\caption{\textbf{Latent chemical axes and competitive pressure predict intervention divergence.} \textbf{a}, Cross-host transfer: predicted versus observed terminal passage ($T_6$) abundance of \textit{Methanomicrobium sp.} (log scale) across 5-bar conditions in Cow OSU and Cow BRRS, evaluated using the model trained solely on Cow OSU. In wet-lab validation, +TMA at 5 bar rescued methanogen colonization in Cow BRRS (23.0\% observed). \textbf{b}, Localized competitive pressure ($z$-score) on \textit{Methanomicrobium sp.} by resident competitors, revealing an insurmountable barrier in Cow OSU ($C_i$: 5.80 to 7.40) versus competitive release in Cow BRRS. \textbf{c}, Inferred metabolic traits in 5-bar latent niche space: affinities for Methanol ($\theta_i^{\text{MeOH}}$) versus Trimethylamine ($\theta_i^{\text{TMA}}$), revealing near-antipodal separation ($150.7^\circ$).}
\label{fig:german_competition}
\end{figure*}

Trained solely on Cow OSU ($K=3$), the model autonomously organized perturbation coordinates without biochemical supervision. Contrast displacement vectors under the 5-bar isobaric regime ($\Delta R_{\text{MeOH, 5b}}$, $\Delta R_{\text{TMA, 5b}}$; Methods) revealed near-antipodal divergence ($\theta = 150.7^\circ$, $\cos\theta = -0.8724$), confirming that Methanol and Trimethylamine define opposing metabolic axes (Fig.~\ref{fig:german_competition}c). Affinity projections revealed that \textit{Methanomicrobium sp.} specializes strongly on TMA ($\theta_i^{\text{TMA}}$) with minimal basal syngas affinity.

Localized competitive pressure ($C_i$; Methods) along preferred resource axes resolved the host divergence (Fig.~\ref{fig:german_competition}b). In Cow OSU, dense resident methylotrophic competitors (\textit{Denitrobacterium detoxificans}, \textit{Bacteroides pyogenes}, \textit{Thermoplasmata}) established an insurmountable barrier ($C_i$ rising from 5.80 to 7.40), driving exclusion. In Cow BRRS, these competitors were depleted, creating competitive release. Consequently, the model trained solely on Cow OSU accurately predicted that +TMA at 5 bar would rescue methanogen colonization in Cow BRRS without recipient recalibration (Fig.~\ref{fig:german_competition}a), matching wet-lab validation where \textit{Methanomicrobium sp.} expanded from unquantifiable baseline traces to $23.0\%$ relative abundance.
