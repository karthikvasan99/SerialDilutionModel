\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Predicting how multispecies microbial communities assemble, fluctuate, and respond to environmental perturbations is a central challenge in microbiome engineering, synthetic biology, and medicine~\cite{venturelli2018deciphering,goldford2018emergent}. Being able to accurately simulate serial dilution and longitudinal community dynamics \textit{in silico} would transform microbial therapeutics: enabling the rational design of defined live biotherapeutic consortia, predicting pathogen exclusion, and forecasting how resident microbiota respond to prebiotic or chemical interventions. The ultimate ambition of microbial community modeling has long been forward dynamic forecasting---taking an initial inoculum $N(0)$ and predicting the temporal trajectory $N(t)$ and multi-passage succession of constituent taxa. 

Yet achieving this has proven extraordinarily difficult. Prevailing modeling frameworks frequently rely on Generalized Lotka-Volterra (gLV) equations and their sophisticated inferential extensions, including regularized dynamic regression~\cite{stein2013ecological}, exhaustive combinatorial co-culture screening~\cite{venturelli2018deciphering}, and Bayesian state-space inference engines such as MDSINE and MDSINE2~\cite{bucci2016mdsine,gibson2025mdsine2}. These frameworks parameterize interspecies interactions through an $S \times S$ phenomenological interaction matrix $\mathbf{A}$. Consequently, existing dynamic models confront a severe double bottleneck. First, quadratic parameter scaling ($O(S^2)$ interaction coefficients) imposes a prohibitive experimental burden: calibrating gLV models demands screening dozens to hundreds of monocultures, pairwise co-cultures, and combinatorial dropout consortia alongside high-frequency longitudinal sampling and absolute biomass quantification (via qPCR, flow cytometry, or spike-in sequencing)~\cite{venturelli2018deciphering,gibson2025mdsine2}. For communities exceeding tens of species, the required combinatorial space quickly becomes experimentally intractable. Second, because gLV interaction coefficients $\alpha_{ij}$ are phenomenological summaries of underlying metabolic and physical exchanges, they are intrinsically condition-specific. Any change in nutrient availability, growth media, or physical microenvironments alters the effective interaction matrix, rendering previously learned parameters obsolete and requiring the entire labor-intensive experimental campaign to be repeated from scratch.

Alternative modeling paradigms have sought to bypass these limitations, yet each has had to make substantial compromises. Mechanistic approaches, such as dynamic Flux Balance Analysis and spatio-temporal metabolic frameworks (COMETS)~\cite{harcombe2014metabolic}, incorporate detailed biochemical stoichiometry, but require labor-intensive, manually curated genome-scale metabolic reconstructions for every individual strain---an intractable requirement for uncultured or non-model isolates---and remain computationally prohibitive for high-throughput combinatorial screens. Confronted by these dual barriers of parameter explosion and experimental fatigue, other pioneering efforts have had to settle for qualitative or statistical approximations: mapping qualitative assembly rules and emergent phases at static endpoints~\cite{friedman2017community,hu2022emergent}, or capturing macroecological abundance fluctuations across wide-ranging biomes without predicting deterministic, strain-resolved trajectories for specific consortia~\cite{ho2022competition}.

Here, we emphasize that our intention is not to propose an entirely new mathematical model. Consumer-Resource Models (CRMs) have served as a cornerstone of theoretical ecology since MacArthur and Tilman~\cite{macarthur1970species,tilman1982resource}, and have long been deployed to theorize, conceptualize, and explore microbial cross-feeding and serial passaging dynamics~\cite{marsland2019available,goldford2018emergent,liao2020modeling}. Rather, our primary thesis is that the profound, untapped power of consumer-resource dynamics lies in their low dimensionality---a foundational principle that has been largely overlooked in data-driven microbiome modeling. 

Building upon our recent insight that complex microbial ecosystems admit low-dimensional coexistence in effective resource coordinates~\cite{srinivasan2026low}, we demonstrate that casting consumer-resource kinetics within a compact latent metabolic space ($K \ll S$) parameterized as a continuous-time Neural ODE~\cite{chen2018neural} fundamentally resolves the bottlenecks of dynamic microbial modeling. By projecting interspecies interactions onto a minimal set of latent resource axes ($K \ll S$), parameter complexity collapses from quadratic $O(S^2)$ scaling to linear $O(S \times K)$ scaling, dramatically reducing experimental data requirements and enabling robust calibration from sparse combinatorial data without overfitting. Furthermore, because consumption affinities $\mathbf{\Theta} \in \mathbb{R}^{S \times K}$ represent cell-autonomous metabolic traits rather than pairwise phenomenological couplings, they can be treated as invariant across environmental contexts, allowing external habitat-specific variations to be decoupled from core metabolic preferences. Finally, by formulating optimization on the probability simplex via discrete Kullback-Leibler divergence, the framework learns directly from standard relative abundance profiles, eliminating the dependency on labor-intensive absolute biomass quantification.

In this work, we demonstrate these capabilities across three distinct experimental benchmarks spanning batch cultures, spatially structured hydrogel metacommunities, and livestock rumen consortia. In a 12-species synthetic human gut community (Venturelli et al.~\cite{venturelli2018deciphering}), our model accurately forecasts combinatorial assembly across 175 consortia over 60 hours using only $K=3$ latent resources, maintaining high predictive fidelity without complexity-dependent performance decay. In a spatially structured experiment where the defined 104-member synthetic human gut community (hCom2; Cheng et al.~\cite{cheng2022design}, Jin et al.~\cite{jin2023culturing}) was serially passaged across physical microenvironments (filtered to 50 prevalent colonizing strains that passed quality-control thresholds), we show that modeling physical transport and boundary dilution kinetics in a coupled two-patch metacommunity enables zero-shot forecasting of community succession on unseen inert hydrogel beads. Finally, in a complex livestock rumen microbiome inoculated from dairy cattle donors (filtered to 81 core bacterial and archaeal taxa; Plata et al.), we find that the learned latent resource coordinates autonomously organize along physical and chemical perturbation axes without biochemical supervision. This reveals near-antipodal latent chemical axes and localized competitive barriers that explain and prospectively prescribe host-divergent intervention outcomes.
